The Afro-American, Baltimore's Black weekly, ran a crossword every Saturday from
December 13, 1924 to March 28, 1925: sixteen puzzles. The record, one row per puzzle, is
in [afroamerican](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/afroamerican).

The puzzle sat on the Magazine Page, whose banner read "EDITED BY AFRO'S WOMAN
EDITOR—EVERY READER OF THIS PAGE IS INVITED TO CONTRIBUTE TO IT". The first ten
puzzles are the paper's own: small hand-ruled grids, three credited to a reader by name
and street (A. B. Koger, 1925-01-10; John E. Cole Jr., 01-24; Georgeanna McMechen,
02-14) and one "submitted by the janitor at School No. 100" (02-07). Solvers sent
answers in for a prize, and the page printed the winner and the honorable mentions two
weeks later with the answers.

The last six (1925-02-21 to 03-28) are in a different, uniform type with "(Copyright,
1925.)" under each grid and no owner named, so they look bought in. Alex DeJarnatt
takes the heart-shaped one of March 7 for the New York World's February 14 puzzle
(see [new-york-world.md](new-york-world.md)); it hasn't been compared with a copy.

This is the first Black newspaper in the census with a crossword run recorded; the
leads note ([research-notes-leads-2026-10-18.md](../research-notes-leads-2026-10-18.md),
section 4) found no prior work on early Black constructors.

Open questions: who the "woman editor" was; who made the six uncredited home-made
puzzles; the source of the six copyrighted ones; whether December 13 was the first (the
puzzle answered that week from the week before is a word rhyme, which points to yes;
the December 6 page hasn't been seen); why it stopped (no crossword found after March
1925 in a search by Alex DeJarnatt, 2026-11-19); whether the paper's other editions or
later years carried one under another heading.
