The Pittsburgh Courier printed "CROSS WORD PUZZLE—NO. 1" on January 17, 1925. The
record, one row per page saved, is in
[pittsburghcourier](https://github.com/EveryPuzzleProject/pittsburghcourier). The pages
saved are samples from the start of each feature, not full runs.

**No. 1 looks home-made**: the grid's numbers are written by hand, the clues are in the
paper's plain type, and no maker or copyright is named. **From No. 2, a week later, every
puzzle carries "(© by Western Newspaper Union.)"** under the Courier's own heading and
numbering (Nos. 2 and 4 to 9 saved, to 1925-03-14). The same syndicate series ran with its
own titles in the Norfolk Journal and Guide
([norfolk-journal-and-guide.md](norfolk-journal-and-guide.md)).

Two later features: from April to at least July 1925, picture-shaped children's
crosswords on a bought-in page, "COURIER COMICS FOR THE KIDDIES", with panels signed Cobb
Shinn; and on 1927-11-05 "Red, White and Blue (CROSS WORD PUZZLES—Two in One)", "Diagram
No. 15", copyright C. M. Brown of Newark, N.J., a novelty with squares in three shades.

Open questions: who made No. 1; how long each feature ran; anything before January 17,
1925; the C. M. Brown series (fifteen diagrams by November 1927, so it began earlier, here
or elsewhere).
